Throughout history, from the Middle Ages to the present day

Spiritual and national leaders across Europe have called their peoples to unite in prayer during critical moments of challenge and decision. These initiatives strengthened social cohesion, inspired hope and faith, and in certain instances played a significant role in events that shaped the destiny of nations and influenced the course of European history.

1571

The Victory at Lepanto

Before the decisive battle between the Christian coalition and the Ottoman Empire, Pope Pius V called all of Europe to prayer.

The victory at Lepanto halted the Ottoman advance into Europe and was widely perceived as an answer to the united prayers of Christians across the continent.

1588

England’s National Prayer During the Spanish Armada Crisis

Queen Elizabeth I called the people of England to prayer and fasting before the invasion of the Spanish Armada.

The defeat of the Armada, aided by severe storms, changed the balance of power in Europe and marked the rise of England as a leading naval power. Days of thanksgiving and prayer subsequently became a national tradition.

18th–19th centuries

The Prayer Movement of the Moravian Brethren

A continuous prayer movement began in 1727 at the estate of Count Zinzendorf in Herrnhut, Germany.

The prayer watch continued uninterrupted for more than a century and became a catalyst for spiritual renewal. This movement laid the foundation for modern Protestant missions, sending thousands of missionaries across the globe and profoundly influencing Christianity worldwide.

1940

United Kingdom — a story of victory

Four days after taking office as prime minister in May 1940, Winston Churchill declared a national day of prayer. People across Britain flocked to local churches and chapels, concerned by news of the German advance on the British Expeditionary Force in Belgium and France. Five days later, news reached the country of the successful evacuation of British troops from Dunkirk.

“You ask me what our policy is? I say: to wage war by sea, by land, and by air, with all the might and power that God gives us; to wage war against a terrible tyranny, which surpasses all human crimes.” Winston Churchill
1950s

The foundation of the European Union

After the devastation of the Second World War, Europe lay in ruins. France and Germany, nations that had been adversaries for centuries, were searching for a path toward reconciliation.

Two Christian statesmen — Robert Schuman of France and Konrad Adenauer of Germany — were deeply committed believers. Before key meetings and important decisions they often prayed together, convinced that Europe’s future should be built not only on political and economic interests, but also on the Christian values of reconciliation, forgiveness and human dignity.

The European Union was born not only at the negotiating table, but also through the shared prayers of Christian leaders who sought God’s wisdom for the reconciliation of the peoples of Europe.

1982–1989

The Leipzig Peace Prayer Movement

The weekly Prayer Meetings for Peace and Freedom held at St. Nicholas Church in Leipzig, East Germany, became one of the defining spiritual forces behind the Peaceful Revolution of 1989.

What began as a small prayer gathering grew into a nationwide movement that inspired peaceful civic action and helped bring about the fall of the Berlin Wall, the reunification of Germany, and the restoration of freedom across Central and Eastern Europe after decades of communist oppression.

1987–1991

The Singing Revolution in Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania

The peaceful movement for independence was marked by mass gatherings where people sang in their native Baltic languages and prayed for freedom.

The movement united the peoples of the Baltic States, helping to restore their independence and inspiring a revival of faith, spiritual life and national identity.

1989

Prayer and Christian resistance in the fall of Romania’s communist regime

The Romanian Revolution of 1989 began in Timișoara, where Pastor László Tőkés became a symbol of resistance.

Churches became places of prayer and peaceful protest, while faith inspired the moral awakening of society. After decades of communist atheism, Romania witnessed a significant spiritual revival alongside its return to freedom.

1991

The collapse of the Soviet Union

Millions of Christians around the world prayed fervently for the fall of the Iron Curtain, the end of the Cold War, and the liberation of nations from totalitarian rule.

The historic events that followed led to the collapse of the communist system, the dissolution of the Soviet Union, and the restoration of freedom for peoples who had longed for independence and the right to determine their own future for decades.

1998

The European Prayer Breakfast

In 1998 a remarkable initiative was launched in Brussels, bringing together European leaders in prayer for peace, freedom and the future of Europe.

The European Prayer Breakfast stands as a powerful reminder that the strength of Europe lies not only in political cooperation and economic prosperity, but also in shared values, human dignity, spiritual wisdom and responsible leadership.

2016

National spiritual event in Poland

On 19 November 2016 a solemn liturgy was held at the Shrine of Divine Mercy in Kraków, during which Polish Catholic bishops proclaimed Jesus Christ “King and Lord” and symbolically recognised Him as King of Poland.

The ceremony was attended by President Andrzej Duda and then-Prime Minister Beata Szydło, as well as tens of thousands of believers. The bishops and the believers present prayed together: “Jesus Christ, reign over us!” This important spiritual event influenced the development of society, culture and even the economy.

2022 — present

Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine

When many experts predicted that Ukraine would quickly fall, millions of people across Ukraine and around the world united in prayer for its protection and survival.

Against all expectations, Ukraine endured and continues to defend its freedom and sovereignty. For many people of faith, the resilience of the Ukrainian nation stands as a powerful testimony to God’s guidance, protection and faithfulness during one of the greatest challenges in its history.

24 February 2026

Joint prayer by European leaders in Kyiv

On 24 February 2026, on the National Day of Prayer, an interfaith prayer service was held at St. Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv, attended by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and his wife Olena, together with high-ranking guests including European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, President of the European Council António Costa, President of Finland Alexander Stubb, Prime Minister of Denmark Mette Frederiksen, Prime Minister of Estonia Kristen Michal, Prime Minister of Iceland Kristrún Frostadóttir, Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, Croatian Prime Minister Andrej Plenković, Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson and Lithuanian Minister of National Defence. The prayer was led by bishops and leaders of churches and religious organisations.

“Today, within the walls of St. Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv, a united prayer for Ukraine was offered. For blessings and assistance for Ukrainian soldiers, for the release of prisoners of war, for every man and woman who was forced to leave their home, for all Ukrainians, and for unity and peace for the Ukrainian people.” President of Ukraine

These stories represent only a small part of Europe’s rich spiritual heritage. Every nation can recall moments when, in times of uncertainty and challenge, people united in prayer, found renewed hope and courage, and witnessed events that shaped the destiny of their society and nation.
