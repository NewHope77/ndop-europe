About the Movement

A continent-wide initiative encouraging every European nation to set aside one day, in law, on which its people may pray together for their country.

Who we are

A movement, not an institution

The National Days of Prayer Movement in Europe brings together churches, religious communities, public figures and ordinary citizens around a single, simple aim.

We are not a denomination, a political party or a lobby group. We are a network of people convinced that the peoples of Europe are strengthened when they are able to pray together for their nations — and that a National Day of Prayer, recognised by the state, gives that prayer a shared date, a shared voice and a lasting place in public life.

Our work is interdenominational by conviction. In every country the initiative belongs to the local churches, communities and leaders of that nation; the movement offers the experience, the precedents and the practical framework that others have already tested.

What guides us

Principles of the movement

Prayer first

Everything the movement does begins and ends with prayer for the nations of Europe — for their freedom, their peace and their common good.

Unity across confessions

The initiative is open to all Christian confessions and, where nations choose it, to interfaith participation. Unity is the point, not uniformity.

Respect for the state

We work through lawful, transparent parliamentary and governmental procedure, with full respect for constitutional order and freedom of religion.

National ownership

Each National Day of Prayer belongs to its own nation — its date, its form and its language are decided locally, by local leaders.

Service to society

A day of prayer is also a day of charity, compassion and service — its fruit is measured in the life of communities.

Open coordination

Materials, legal precedents and contacts are shared freely with anyone ready to carry the initiative in their country.

What we do

From an idea to a day in the national calendar

We gather and share the evidence

Europe’s history holds many moments in which united prayer preceded decisive change. The movement documents these stories, together with the legal texts and parliamentary procedures that turned a day of prayer into law in the United States and in Ukraine.

We connect people across borders

The movement introduces church leaders, parliamentarians and civic initiators to counterparts who have already walked this road, so that no national committee has to begin from a blank page.

We accompany national initiatives

From the first interdenominational meeting to the drafting of a legislative proposal and the celebration of the first National Day of Prayer, the movement offers a seven-step framework tested in practice.

  • Support in forming an interdenominational steering committee.
  • Model documents, vision papers and legislative language.
  • Guidance on engaging government and national leaders.
  • Materials for the first national celebration.
